Understanding changes in vitamin D levels during the COVID-19 pandemic is crucial due to its impact on bone health, immunity, and overall well-being. \cite{Holick2011}, \cite{Aranow2011}, \cite{Manson2019}. As vitamin D deficiency is associated with numerous adverse health outcomes, including increased susceptibility to infections and autoimmune diseases, it is very important to examine how the pandemic may have influenced existing nutritional deficiencies. In this study, we observed a significant decline in mean vitamin D levels and a corresponding increase in deficiency rates in the Bavarian population during the pandemic. These findings, consistent across multiple analytical methods, including descriptive statistics, PSM, and machine learning models, underscore the robustness of the observed trends. Specifically, mean vitamin D levels decreased from 26.7 µg/l to 25.9 µg/l, and deficiency rates rose from 31.2\% to 35.5\% during the pandemic, with the strongest effect observed in middle-aged adults. 

\textbf{The pandemic and vitamin D levels}
The pandemic significantly reduced vitamin D levels across all demographics, with middle-aged adults being most affected (40–59 years). These findings align with the general trend reported in other populations globally. Reduced outdoor activity and limited sunlight exposure during lockdowns were among the key contributing factors to this decline. For instance, several studies were focusing on the vitamin D status of  children and reported lower levels during the pandemic, likely due to confinement measures. \cite{Yu2020},\cite{Kilic2023}, \cite{Wong2021} The reduction in outdoor activities is suspected to be the main reason for decreased sun exposure, which is essential for maintaining adequate vitamin D levels. 

Fewer studies have looked at the pandemic's effect on vitamin D levels in adults. One study found that young women in Japan also faced lower vitamin D levels during the pandemic, supporting the notion that reduced sun exposure was a widespread issue \cite{Tsugawa2022}. Also among hospitalized COVID-19 patients researchers found an increase in vitamin D deficiency rates, suggesting that deficiency may also be exacerbated by the illness itself \cite{Smaha2023}. Some studies also focused on vitamin D levels among other vulnerable groups during the pandemic, such as pediatric patients with chronic kidney disease and individuals with pre-existing vitamin D deficiencies and found significant declines in vitamin D levels across these cohorts \cite{ParraOrtega2023}, \cite{Katz2020}.

The majority of the literature concerning vitamin D status and COVID-19 has focused on the correlation between vitamin D deficiency and adverse COVID-19 outcomes. While this relationship is well-documented—numerous studies have suggested that lower vitamin D levels could potentially worsen COVID-19 symptoms \cite{Weir2020} - another significant aspect is the increased awareness of vitamin D’s importance during the pandemic. This awareness, driven by extensive media coverage and public health discussions, led many to recognize the role of vitamin D in supporting immune function, prompting higher rates of supplementation\cite{Kwon2022}. As a result, not only did reduced sunlight exposure during lockdowns play a role, but heightened awareness likely influenced the vitamin D status in the Bavarian adult population. Interestingly, our results showed that younger adults aged 18–39 did not exhibit an increase in vitamin D deficiency rates, \ref{tab:Table 3} despite being tested more frequently during the pandemic than before \ref{tab:Table 1}. This may reflect a trend in which this segment of the population became more aware of and proactive about micronutrient supplementation, as highlighted in both scientific literature and media sources during the pandemic. \cite{Lowry2015} 

\textbf{Variations across different age groups}
Moving from the broader observations of vitamin D levels comparing before and during the COVID-19 pandemic, our analysis reveals significant age-related variations. The largest decrease in vitamin D levels was observed in middle-aged adults (40–59 years), with this group also experiencing the most pronounced increase in deficiency rates. In contrast, younger adults (18–39 years) showed a slighter decline in vitamin D levels, with minimal changes in deficiency rates. In our study, older adults (60+ years), although showing a significant decline in mean vitamin D levels, had also relatively lower deficiency rates compared to middle-aged adults. 

There is no clear consensus in the literature regarding which adult age groups have the highest prevalence of vitamin D deficiency. One study found that vitamin D deficiency is most prevalent among the elderly and/or disabled individuals in residential care, likely due to limited sun exposure. \cite{Nowson2012} These findings align with an observational study on an older Irish population, which found that vitamin D deficiency was particularly prevalent among those over 80 years of age, with the highest deficiency rates observed in this group. \cite{Laird2018} Additionally, a meta-analysis revealed that nearly 20\% of middle-aged and older adults in the United States were vitamin D deficient. \cite{Wang2023} Regarding COVID-19 infection and vitamin D levels across age groups, it has become evident that vitamin D deficiency in older adults significantly increases the risk of worse COVID-19 outcomes. \cite{Dror2021}, \cite{Dramé2021}, \cite{Sulli2021} 

These findings underscore the complex relationship between age, vitamin D status, and health outcomes, particularly during the pandemic. Given the heightened vulnerability of both older and middle-aged adults, targeted public health interventions may be necessary to mitigate vitamin D deficiency in these groups, especially in the context of infectious diseases like COVID-19.

\textbf{Differences comparing males and females}
Our analysis revealed that both males and females experienced a decline in vitamin D levels during the pandemic, with males showing a slightly stronger increase in deficiency rates. This trend is consistent with a study conducted in the Israeli population, which found a higher prevalence of vitamin D deficiency among males during the pandemic. \cite{Merzon2020}Similarly, researchers identified a higher rate of vitamin D deficiency in male COVID-19 patients, which was associated with more severe outcomes. \cite{DeSmet2020} 

Conversely, a systematic review and meta-analysis found that low vitamin D levels were significantly associated with an increased risk of COVID-19 infection, with a stronger correlation in female patients . \cite{Mishra2022} Moreover, non-COVID-related research on gender-specific differences in vitamin D levels suggests that concentrations tend to be lower in females. This may be attributed to a strong link between vitamin D deficiency and obesity, as females generally have higher fat mass, which could lead to lower vitamin D concentrations. \cite{Muscogiuri2019}, \cite{Alquaiz2018}

Additionally, we observed a lower testing rate for vitamin D levels in males compared to females, which could suggest that males are less likely to seek preventive care or awareness around vitamin D status, while females may be more proactive in getting tested. This aligns with previous research showing that men are less likely to recognize disease symptoms and tend to use primary care services less frequently than women, which may contribute to delayed diagnosis and treatment in men. \cite{Baker2016} As a result, males might only seek testing when deficiency-related symptoms become severe, potentially increasing their risk for more adverse outcomes.

Overall, while both genders were affected by the pandemic in terms of declining vitamin D levels, males appear to be more vulnerable to deficiency-related severe COVID-19 outcomes, while females may face additional risk factors due to physiological differences. These findings stress the need for gender-specific approaches in addressing vitamin D deficiency, especially in the context of public health interventions during pandemics.

\textbf{Seasonal Influence}
As expected, seasonality emerged as the most influential factor affecting vitamin D levels in our analysis, explaining 70.2\% of the variance in levels based on the machine learning feature importance analysis \ref{fig:feature_importance}). Spring was the most impacted season during the pandemic, with a significant worsening in vitamin D levels observed during this time. 

Many previous studies report about the strong influence of season on vitamin D levels across all population groups. \cite{Klenk2013}, \cite{Kasahara2013}, \cite{Romero-Ortuño2011} These seasonal variations in vitamin D levels have also been reported during the COVID-19 pandemic. \cite{Tsugawa2022}, \cite{Li2022}. One study utilizing an interrupted time series design examined the impact of the pandemic on vitamin D levels in toddlers and infants, finding that the typical seasonal fluctuations in vitamin D were diminished due to pandemic-related lockdowns. \cite{Wong2021} While their focus was on a different age group, our analysis similarly observed this smoothing of seasonal variation in vitamin D levels. 

In the sensitivity analysis that we conducted, spring emerged as the season most significantly affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. \ref{tab:5} Prior to the pandemic, several studies consistently reported that vitamin D levels tend to be lowest during late winter and early spring months \cite{Klenk2013}, \cite{Kasahara2013}. This can be explained by the reduced sunlight exposure during the winter, leading to depleted vitamin D stores by the time spring arrives. With pandemic-related restrictions limiting outdoor activities during these critical months, the opportunity to replenish vitamin D levels through sun exposure was further diminished. Consequently, the pandemic likely exacerbated the typical seasonal deficiencies, particularly in the spring, when sunlight exposure is crucial for recovery from wintertime depletion.

\textbf{Strengths of our analysis in assessing impact using real-world data (RWD)}
One of the major strengths of our analysis lies in the utilization of real-world data (RWD), which provides a large-scale, representative sample from a diverse population. Unlike controlled clinical studies, which often involve specific populations under strict experimental conditions, RWD allows for the exploration of health trends in a more naturalistic, everyday context. By leveraging data from 301,760 individuals, we were able to capture population-level trends, which is crucial in understanding how environmental and social factors, such as lockdowns and restricted outdoor activity, affected vitamin D status during the pandemic. The study sample might not be a perfect mirror of the general German population, particularly due to the overrepresentation of older adults and women. \cite{destatis_population_2024} However, it is representative of the population that would be more likely to get tested for vitamin D levels—namely, older individuals and women, who tend to engage more in preventive health measures. The differences align with expected patterns in healthcare utilization rather than population demographics.

RWD is particularly advantageous for epidemiological research because it provides an extensive dataset that allows for an especially effective integration of machine learning techniques to draw robust inferences from the data. As previously noted in the literature, machine learning has increasingly become an invaluable tool in epidemiology, offering a powerful tool to tackle Big Data problems for which classical methods are not as well-suited. \cite{Bi2019}, \cite{Rahman2023}
Statistical methods and machine learning allow for greater flexibility in epidemiological analysis by relaxing the strict assumptions required by traditional models. Data mining and machine learning can offer new insights into disease progression by uncovering non-linear relationships that conventional statistical models might overlook. \cite{Lynch2016}, \cite{Balzer2021}, \cite{Wang2019} In our study, the ML model helped us account for confounders and showcase the robustness of our findings from the traditional analytical approaches, such as the Propensity Score Matching.

Despite its many advantages, there are still several methodological challenges associated with using RWD and ML techniques. These include potential data quality issues, bias, and regulatory concerns. Especially the diversity and  heterogeneity of RWD sources can lead to inconsistencies in data accuracy and completeness.  \cite{Grimberg2021} 
This is particularly relevant to our study, which utilizes a large laboratory dataset from the German primary healthcare system. Ensuring consistency and high data quality through proper standardization protocols is crucial for robust analysis. \cite{Cave2019}, \cite{Bernal-Delgado2019}  The Honic data platform employs a data model based on an Extract-Transform-Load (ETL) approach to guarantee interoperability and standardization across all data providers on the platform. \cite{honic} The Extract, Transform, Load (ETL) process involves extracting data from various sources, transforming it into a suitable format by cleaning and structuring it, and then loading it into a target database or data warehouse for analysis.

There are inherent limitations of using RWD in healthcare, such as difficulties in controlling for confounding variables. \cite{Rudrapatna2020} However, these challenges are balanced by the significant opportunities that RWD presents for large-scale, real-world impact assessments, such as the one we conducted. The benefits of using RWD in enhancing patient care can outweigh the limitations, especially when it comes to analyzing health outcomes across large populations, as we did in our study. \cite{Ho2019} The value of RWD in our study is evident in the way it allowed us to examine the pandemic’s impact on vitamin D levels across a broad spectrum of demographic groups, providing insights that would have been difficult to achieve through traditional clinical studies alone.

\textbf{Implications for public health}
The observed decline in vitamin D levels during the COVID-19 pandemic highlights significant public health concerns related to lifestyle changes, such as reduced outdoor activity and limited sunlight exposure. These findings reveal the need for targeted interventions, particularly for vulnerable groups like middle-aged adults as well as elderly and those with restricted sun exposure. \cite{Smaha2023}, \cite{Weir2020}
Our findings also underscore the importance of vitamin D supplementation, regular monitoring and proactive healthcare measures during periods of confinement and for vulnerable populations at greater risk from vitamin D deficiency, for example those living in geographical locations with reduced sunlight exposure. \cite{Katz2020}

\textbf{Study limitations}
While our analysis leverages RWD to offer insights into the pandemic's impact on vitamin D levels, there are inherent limitations. The dataset may contain variations in  completeness and accuracy of lab measurements across different laboratory facilities. However, it is important to note that the potential variation in lab measurements is minimized in this study, as all Vitamin D data comes from a single laboratory chain. The measurements are highly standardized across all labs in the chain, using the same methods, units, and ranges, ensuring consistency in the Vitamin D parameter across the dataset. 
The external validity of our findings is limited, as the data is specific to Bavaria, and may not be generalizable to other regions with differing healthcare systems, climates, or demographic characteristics. Despite using advanced methods like propensity score matching and machine learning to account for confounders, the observational nature of RWD makes it difficult to establish direct causality between the pandemic and vitamin D deficiencies.

\textbf{Broader impact}
In conclusion, our findings underscore the significant public health implications of vitamin D deficiency, especially during extended periods of restricted activity like the COVID-19 pandemic. Addressing vitamin D deficiency should be prioritized in public health strategies, particularly for vulnerable populations, to mitigate the risks posed by future pandemics or lockdowns. It's likely that other essential nutrients were also affected, suggesting that comprehensive monitoring of nutritional deficiencies is critical. The use of RWD enables large-scale tracking of such trends in real time, highlighting its potential for future pandemics. For future health crises, greater attention must be given to both monitoring deficiencies and implementing timely interventions to protect public health.
